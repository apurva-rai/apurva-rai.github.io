Research

Secure messaging and web connections usually rely on a trusted third party, such as a certificate authority or an app’s key server, to vouch for who is on the other end. My research designs and proves protocols that remove that trust: people authenticate a connection themselves by comparing a short string, and security holds even if the server is compromised.

With my coauthors, I have built SAS-based authentication for two-party key exchange (ASIACRYPT 2025), for Signal’s X3DH handshake while preserving its deniability (SCN 2026), and for group key agreement without adding protocol messages (CANS 2026). I also work on password- and biometric-based key exchange, including an asymmetric fuzzy PAKE that protects the biometric data a server stores, which I implemented and benchmarked. I expect to complete my Ph.D. in June 2027.
